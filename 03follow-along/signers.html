Who were the signers

Famous Signers

Some of the most famous signers of the Declaration of Independence include:

John Hancock (Massachusetts)
Best known for his large, flamboyant signature, which has made his name a synonym for "signature" in American English. He was president of the Continental Congress when the document was adopted.
Thomas Jefferson (Virginia)
The principal author of the Declaration. He later served as the third President of the United States.
Benjamin Franklin (Pennsylvania)
The oldest signer, at 70. He was a renowned scientist, writer, inventor, and diplomat, and he served on the committee that drafted the document.
John Adams (Massachusetts)
A leading advocate for independence in Congress who also served on the drafting committee. He became the second President.
Samuel Adams (Massachusetts)
A key organizer of colonial resistance, associated with the Sons of Liberty and the Boston Tea Party.
Benjamin Rush (Pennsylvania)
A physician and social reformer, often considered one of the leading medical figures of his era.
Charles Carroll of Carrollton (Maryland)
The only Catholic signer and the last surviving signer, dying in 1832.
Richard Henry Lee (Virginia)
Introduced the resolution for independence that Congress voted on in June 1776.
Robert Morris (Pennsylvania)
A wealthy merchant who helped finance the Revolution.
Button Gwinnett (Georgia)
Well known today mainly because his autograph is extremely rare and valuable among collectors, since he died only a few months after signing.

Two of the signers, Jefferson and Adams, famously both died on July 4, 1826, the 50th anniversary of the Declaration's adoption.

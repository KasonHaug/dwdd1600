Digital Citizenship

What is Digital Citizenship?

Digital citizenship is the practice of using technology and the internet responsibly, ethically, and safely. It covers how people behave online and how they engage with digital communities, much like good citizenship in a physical community.

What is Digital Law?

Digital law is the body of laws, regulations, and legal principles that govern how people and organizations use technology, the internet, and digital information. In digital citizenship frameworks, "digital law" is also one of the commonly taught elements, focused on the ethical and legal responsibilities of being online.

What is AUP?

An Acceptable Use Policy is a set of rules that spells out how people may and may not use a network, device, website, or service. Whoever owns or provides the technology writes it, and users usually have to agree to it before getting access.

What is plagiarism?

Plagiarism is presenting someone else's words, ideas, or work as your own without giving proper credit. It's a form of academic and professional dishonesty, and it connects to the copyright and digital law topics you've been looking at.

What is academic dishonesty?

Academic dishonesty is any act of deception or unfair advantage in schoolwork, testing, or research. It's the broader category that plagiarism belongs to, and it goes against the idea of being honest and responsible in how you use information and technology.

What are the rules for AI generated content?

Rules for AI-generated content vary by place and context: the EU now requires disclosure of AI-generated deepfakes and chatbots, the US has no single federal disclosure law (just a patchwork of state laws) and won't copyright works made without sufficient human authorship, and platforms, workplaces, and schools set their own policies. The safest approach is to follow your school's or employer's AI rules, disclose AI use when required or when hiding it would mislead, and never pass off AI output as entirely your own work.
